I used `pull()` to try to calculate the mean of each variable. The mean
worked for `num_var`. It also worked for `log_var` because R treats
`TRUE` as 1 and `FALSE` as 0, so the result is the proportion of sampled
values greater than zero. The means of `char_var` and `factor_var` did
not work and returned missing values.

``` r
as.numeric(pull(p2_df, log_var))
as.numeric(pull(p2_df, char_var))
as.numeric(pull(p2_df, factor_var))
```

After converting the variables to numeric, `log_var` became 1 for `TRUE`
and 0 for `FALSE`, which explains why its mean worked. Converting
`char_var` produced missing values because R can’t interpret words like
`"one"` and `"two"` as numbers. Converting `factor_var` produced the
codes 1, 2, and 3 for its levels, but these are just category labels, so
taking their mean doesn’t make much sense.
